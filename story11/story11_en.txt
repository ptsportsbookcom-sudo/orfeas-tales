New Friends for Rilaki

Once upon a time, Theotokis and Aristotelis decided it was time to go see their friends — Rillas, Rilena, and their son, Rilaki. It had been far too long since their last visit. In the meantime, the monkeys had come back too — Maimudakis and Maimudakena — and when the boys asked, they happily agreed to come along, bringing with them two little boys of their own, Dakis and Akis.

They hopped on their bikes and set off — vroom, vroom, vroom! — and pulled up outside Rillas's door. They knocked. "Hey, Rillas!" Nothing. They knocked again. This time a little gorilla popped his head out. "Hey, look who's here!" "Wait — who are you?" "It's me, Rilaki! Oh my goodness, it's Theotokis and Aristotelis! You haven't visited in so long, I've grown so much! Where are my dads?" "They're sleeping." "Sleeping? Let's go wake them up!" "They're having a siesta. Okay, okay."

They found Rillas groggy and half-asleep. "Ugh, guys... why did you wake us up? You haven't come to see us in ages!" "Wait, wait — the monkeys haven't even met your son yet. It's the perfect chance, let's do it today." "Okay, okay, let's go."

They started climbing into the gorilla-cars, but stopped. "No, wait — let's go wake up the monkeys too, so they can come along." Everyone piled into the gorilla-cars and the monkey-cars, and off they all went — vroom, vroom, vroom!

They arrived at the monkeys' house. The kids spotted the monkeys, ducked away to hide for a moment, then came out to help. "Hey there, monkey!" "Oh, hey guys, what's up?" "We came to see you for a bit." While the grown-ups chatted, the kids noticed a big pile of bananas lying around. Bored and full of energy, they started chucking bananas at each other, laughing as they ducked and dodged behind the trees. Suddenly, from somewhere deep inside a tree, an old, gravelly voice called out: "Who's throwing the bananas?"

The kids kept right on throwing — whoosh, splat, whoosh — darting behind trees so no one would catch them in the act. But the monkey wasn't fooled. He marched over, grabbed the two little culprits by the ears, and pulled them out of hiding. "Come out of there, come on out!" Out tumbled two little rascals — Dakis and Akis! Rillas, Rilena, and Rilaki said hello and introduced Aristotelis and Theotokis as their "two very naughty friends." But Dakis and Akis crossed their arms. "We don't want them. They're not good friends." "Why not, guys? What did they ever do to you?" "Bad friends. We don't like them. We don't like them at all."

So Theotokis decided to win them over. "I have a magic trick to show you!" "Okay, okay, let's see it!" "Rilaki, Dakis, Akis — go stand in a line." He grabbed one banana... then a second... then a third — and started juggling them! "Whoa, magic!" the kids shouted, thrilled to bits.

"I have a trick too," said Aristotelis, and whispered a secret to the monkey. He and Theotokis walked over and stood beside a big tree. Theotokis held up a sheet in front of Aristotelis and counted, "One, two, three..." and dropped it. Aristotelis was gone! "Oh my goodness!" The kids' minds were blown — they had no idea he'd simply slipped behind the tree, and once they figured it out, they burst out laughing.

"One more trick," Theotokis announced. "I'll make Rillas and the monkey disappear!" The kids' jaws dropped. He did the same trick with the sheet — "One, two, three... ta-da!" — and Rillas and the monkey vanished (they had simply ducked out of sight). The kids went wild. "Bravo, bravo! You're the best!"

Then Rilaki had an idea. "Should we cause a little trouble?" "Yes! Remember those bananas?" "Let's have a banana war!" Dakis, Akis, and Rilaki teamed up and started flinging bananas everywhere. The grown-up gorillas grabbed a shield off one of the motorbikes — bam, bam! — and crept forward together in a line. Then, all at once, they shouted "Attack!" and closed in from every side, surrounding Dakis, Akis, and Rilaki completely.

The kids had to admit it — grown-ups really were a little bit smarter after all! But more than anything, it was the start of a beautiful friendship between Dakis, Akis, and Rilaki.

They spent the whole afternoon playing and laughing together, until at last the sky went dark. "It's gotten late," said Aristotelis and Theotokis. "Time to head home — thanks for everything, guys!" They fired up the bikes — vroom, vroom, vroom, vroom! — and rode off home. Everyone went to sleep. "Goodnight, kids." "Goodnight, monkey."
