Lost Friends, Found Friends

Once upon a time, Rilaki went out to play with his friends Dakis and Akis. They ran and laughed through the jungle, enjoying their game so much that they wandered farther and farther away. When they finally stopped and looked around, they realized they were lost.

"Oh no! What are we going to do?" asked Rilaki.

"It'll be alright," said Dakis. "Let's keep going this way. We are bound to find something."

"But we don't know where we're going," Rilaki replied.

"Come on," said Dakis. "Have faith!"

So the three friends continued walking, even as night began to fall.

Back home, Rillas stepped outside and called, "Rilaki! Rilaki! Come home!" But there was no answer.

Maimoudakis came out too and called for Dakis and Akis. Then he went to Rillas.

"Where can they be?" he asked. "They were all together."

"Let's wait a little longer," said Rillas. "Perhaps they lost track of time."

They waited for an hour, but the children did not return. Now the jungle was dark, and it was becoming dangerous.

"We need Theotokis and Aristotelis," said Rillas. He called them and explained that the children had disappeared again, just as they had once done while playing hide-and-seek.

Theotokis and Aristotelis grabbed their flashlights at once. Together with Rillas and Maimoudakis, they began searching the dark jungle.

Meanwhile, Rilaki, Dakis, and Akis were exhausted. They found a sheltered place beneath a tree and tried to rest. In the darkness, they did not notice the enormous elephant sleeping nearby.

A branch cracked beneath one of their feet.

The huge elephant opened his eyes and rose angrily.

"Who woke me up?" he roared. "You will pay for this! I'm tired of being disturbed!"

The three friends stared up at him in terror.

"Oh my goodness! He's going to get us!" they cried.

They ran through the jungle shouting for help, while the furious elephant chased after them.

Not far away, Ntakis heard their voices.

"That sounds like my friend Rilaki," he said. "It is Rilaki!"

Ntakis hurried toward the cries. When Rilaki, Dakis, and Akis saw him, they rushed over and hid behind him.

The large elephant thundered after them—but suddenly Rillas burst through the trees and shouted, "Stop!"

Ntakis stood bravely in front of his frightened friends.

"Leave them alone," he said. "They saved me from the hole before. You will not hurt them!"

The large elephant tried to argue, but Ntakis insisted that everything had been a mistake. Then Ntina, Ntakis's mother, appeared and calmly took charge.

"Enough," she said. "These children are our friends."

The children apologized for waking the elephant. The elephant accepted their apology and went away, and at last everyone could breathe again.

"We got lost," Dakis explained. "Do you know where our home is?"

"I do," said Ntakis, "but finding the way at night will be difficult."

Ntina invited the tired children to stay safely with her and Ntakis until morning.

Elsewhere in the jungle, Theotokis and Aristotelis had still found no sign of their friends.

"We must not panic," said Theotokis. "We need a plan. Some of us should rest while Aristotelis and I keep watch. As soon as the sun rises, we'll search again."

The long night finally passed. At first light, everyone set out once more.

Theotokis soon noticed small footprints in the dirt.

"These tracks look familiar," he said. "Let's follow them."

The trail led through the jungle and stopped at the entrance to a cave.

"What if a tiger has caught them?" someone whispered.

With their flashlights ready, they carefully entered.

Inside, they found Ntakis standing protectively in front of Rilaki, Dakis, and Akis.

"They're alright," Ntakis explained. "They're my friends. They were very frightened last night because another elephant was chasing them, so my mother and I kept them safe."

Relief swept through the cave. The children had been found, and everyone was safe. Soon Ntina and the others gathered together, and the tired friends woke to a joyful reunion.

"Thank you," said Rillas to Ntina and Ntakis.

"You should thank the little elephant," Ntina replied. "He protected your children because they once protected him. That is what friends do."

"Would you like to come home with us and celebrate with bananas?" asked Rillas.

"Bananas!" cried Dakis and Akis. "Oh my goodness—yes, please!"

They climbed onto the elephants' backs and happily made their way home. Everyone celebrated the children's safe return, grateful that old friends and new friends had looked after one another.

And they all lived happily ever after.
