The Bird of Knowledge

Once upon a time, Rillas woke up to a terrible noise. He heard bulldozers, hammers, drills, and chainsaws. "Oh my goodness, what is going on?" he wondered. He went outside and saw a construction site in the distance — they had orders to cut down every tree in the jungle! "Oh my goodness, oh my goodness! What am I going to do, what am I going to do?"

He called Theotokis and Aristotelis right away. "Come quick, it's urgent! Mayday, mayday! They're going to destroy our jungle!" "Oh no!" the two boys shouted. "Let's go right now, bring the quad bikes!" Vroom-vroom-vroom, they reached Rillas. "They're coming to destroy the jungle!" he explained. "Let's go ask them what's going on," said Theotokis.

They went up to the workers. "What do you want, boys?" "We have orders to cut down every tree — we're building here." "But this is a jungle, it's protected!" "If you can't find a protected species, we can't stop. There are gorillas and monkeys everywhere, they'll just move." "Can you wait a day or two?" "No, no."

Theotokis called a lawyer friend of his. "Come on, Eteoklis, we need help in the jungle, right now!" Eteoklis jumped in his car and arrived at once. He looked over the workers' papers. "This paper isn't right — it's the wrong article, you need to bring the correct one!" The site foreman got annoyed. "Fine. We'll see about that."

Eteoklis went to Theotokis and Aristotelis. "Boys, there's one hope — if you can find a protected species, we can save the jungle. But in seven days they'll be back, and after that there's nothing more we can do." "Okay, okay."

They went to Rillas, Rilena, and Rilaki. "Let us go home to get organized, and we'll be right back!" They got home, Orfeas man. "What are we going to do, what are we going to find?" Theotokis asked. "Let's get on the internet, see if anything turns up."

He searched and searched, and found an article: that very jungle had once been home to the Bird of Knowledge — a bird that knew everything, with beautiful orange, blue, and red feathers. But no one had seen it in five hundred years! "We have to find this Bird of Knowledge!" said Theotokis. "Tomorrow we'll go ask our friends."

The next day they went to Rillas. "Have you ever seen this bird?" "No," said Rillas. "Not me either," said Rilena. "Nor me," said Rilaki. They asked Maimudakis and Maimudakena too — "No, no." Then Dakis spoke up: "Akis says he remembers something!" "Seriously? Five hundred years, guys!" "This bird does ring a bell," said Akis. "I saw it a few months ago, it was really impressive. But it saw me and ran off, hopped away."

Theotokis got back online. "This Bird of Knowledge is very afraid of humans and monkeys — but Akis is little, maybe it'll want to play with him! And you know what this magic bird eats? Books!" "What? It eats books?" "But to eat a book, it can't have already read it — it reads them first, then eats them. And since it's the Bird of Knowledge, it's probably read every book there is!"

Then Akis said, "Now that you mention it... I wrote a book, Strongman! Nobody else has ever read it — I wrote it and drew all the pictures myself!" "So that book doesn't exist anywhere else!" "Do you have it with you?" "Yes, I do!" "I have an idea," said Theotokis. "We'll set a trap for the Bird of Knowledge — but just to film it, we don't need to catch it!"

"How will it work?" "We'll go to the tree where Akis saw it, and have him read Strongman out loud." "But Akis," they warned him, "there's a chance the bird might take your book and eat it." "No! I worked so hard on it!" "Do you want them to destroy our homes, your friends' homes?" "Yes, but Strongman is Strongman! I'm not giving him up!"

Rillas and Maimudakis pulled him aside. "Come on, buddy. If it comes to that, we'll make you a new Strongman — we'll all remember the story together and tell it back to you so you can write it again. Okay?" "Okay."

For the next two days, Rilaki and Dakis learned the story of Strongman by heart, right alongside Akis, just in case. Sunday arrived, Orfeas man — a very critical day, because on Monday the construction crew would come to destroy everything. "Oh my goodness, oh my goodness!"

They went to the tree, hid, and set up cameras everywhere to catch the Bird of Knowledge. Akis began reading aloud: "Once upon a time there lived a gentleman named Strongman. He was the Wrong Man — whatever he did, he did it wrong. One day he saw an injustice happening in the street and went to help. He stumbled, because he did everything wrong — but with his stumble he headbutted the bad guy and defeated him! From that day on, they called him Strongman!"

As he read, they heard something far off: "Frrrr... frrrr... whoosh... whoosh..." "I hear something!" Rillas whispered. "Shh, don't talk!" said Theotokis. "If the Bird of Knowledge notices us, it's all over!" Akis kept reading... and suddenly — click! — the Bird of Knowledge landed right beside him. It was enormous, Orfeas man, with gorgeous orange, blue, and red feathers!

The bird sniffed at Akis's book. "Do you want it?" Akis asked it. "Yes!" answered the Bird of Knowledge. "I don't know who this Strongman is, and it's fascinating! Will you give it to me?" Akis took a deep breath. "For you... I'll make this sacrifice." He handed over the book, and the Bird of Knowledge ate it and learned everything about Strongman.

"Thank you for this beautiful gift, Akis," said the bird. "In exchange, I'll give you one of my feathers. If you ever truly need me, throw it in the air — and I'll be there." And with that, it flew away.

"We got it all on camera!" Akis shouted happily. "Now we wait for tomorrow."

The next day the construction foreman arrived, all puffed up, with Eteoklis beside him. "Ha ha! I've got the right paper now — I'm going to destroy this whole jungle!" "Watch this video first," Eteoklis told him. The man watched the video and turned green, then red. "Unfortunately, we can't destroy this jungle — the Bird of Knowledge lives here!"

Panic! A party! "They'll never destroy us!" everyone cheered happily. "And thank you too, Eteoklis, for saving our jungle!"

They all went home happy and slept soundly. And they lived happily ever after — and we... even better!
