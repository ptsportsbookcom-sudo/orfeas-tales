Planet 650

One sunny Friday, Aristotelis called Theotokis on the phone, bursting with excitement. "Hey! Let's go find the Chief on planet Aphrodite! Remember the spaceship he left us?"

Theotokis laughed. "Alright, let's go! But we can't go alone — let's ask Rillas, Rilaki, Maimoudakis, Dakis, and Akis if they want to come too."

"Great idea! We'll surprise him — let's not tell him we're coming!"

The next morning, the boys hopped on their quad bikes and rode straight to Rillas's house. "Guess what," Aristotelis grinned, "we're going on a trip — to Aphrodite, to visit the Chief!"

Rillas's eyes lit up. "An expedition to Aphrodite? Count us all in!" Rilaki cheered, Maimoudakis stroked his moustache thoughtfully, and Dakis and Akis were already bouncing with excitement.

Everyone climbed aboard the Chief's spaceship. Aristotelis jumped into the pilot's seat. "Don't worry, I'm the best pilot there is!"

"Are you sure you know what you're doing?" asked Theotokis nervously, watching Aristotelis press button after button.

"Relax! I've got this!" Aristotelis pressed one more button just to see what it did — and with a giant WHOOSH, the ship rocketed off the ground and shot straight up into space!

They zoomed past planet after planet. "Is this it?" Aristotelis kept asking. "No, no, that's not it either," Theotokis kept answering, checking the coordinates on the ship's little computer.

Finally, they landed on a planet that looked just like Aphrodite. "We made it!" Aristotelis cheered.

But before anyone could celebrate, another spaceship swooped down out of the sky. Its doors slid open, and out stepped an alien with a blaster raised high. "Freeze! Who are you, and what are you doing on my planet?"

The boys froze, hands in the air. "Please don't shoot!" squeaked Theotokis. "We're so sorry — could you tell us which planet this is?"

"This is Planet 650," said the alien. "Now tell me the truth, or I'll take you all to prison!"

"My name is Theotokis, and this is Aristotelis. We're not enemies — we're from planet Earth! We were trying to reach our friend the Chief on Aphrodite, but our pilot," he glanced at Aristotelis, "got us a little lost."

"Hey! I got the coordinates right — somebody must have bumped the controls!" Aristotelis protested.

The alien lowered her blaster slightly, curious now. "I am Arxigeena. I've heard of Earth, but never visited. And Aphrodite is much nicer than this rainy old rock, believe me."

Theotokis introduced everyone — Rillas, Rilaki, Maimoudakis, Dakis, and Akis — and explained they only wanted to surprise their friend the Chief.

Arxigeena thought for a moment, then smiled. "Here's the deal. Help me finish today's chores on Planet 650, and afterward, I'll fly you all to Aphrodite myself."

"Deal!" everyone agreed at once.

It rained the whole day on Planet 650. The friends hauled heavy stones, scrubbed the slippery rain-soaked terraces clean, and gathered baskets full of strange glowing alien fruit. By evening, everyone was completely worn out — but the work was done.

"Come on, climb aboard," said Arxigeena, and her spaceship lifted off into the starry sky, heading for Aphrodite at last.

Partway through the flight, Theotokis picked up the radio. "Come in, Chief — this is Theotokis, over! We're almost at Aphrodite — we're coming to visit!"

The Chief, fast asleep, jolted awake at the crackling voice. "Wait — you're already on your way?! Why didn't anyone warn me?"

"Surprise!" shouted Aristotelis.

Moments later, Arxigeena's ship touched down on Aphrodite. The boys climbed out first, followed by Rillas, Rilaki, Maimoudakis, Dakis, and Akis. "We brought a friend along too," Theotokis said with a smile — and Arxigeena stepped down the ramp behind them.

The Chief froze. He stared at Arxigeena as if he had never seen anything so wonderful in his whole life. "Oh my goodness," he whispered. "I don't believe I have ever seen anyone quite like you."

"This is Arxigeena, from Planet 650," Theotokis explained. "She rescued us when we got lost, and she's the reason we made it here at all."

"Well then," said the Chief, bowing grandly, "welcome to Aphrodite, Arxigeena. It is truly the loveliest planet in the galaxy — though I must say, it just got a little lovelier."

That evening, everyone celebrated together. They ate the Chief's favourite feast, played games under two shimmering moons, and gathered baskets of sparkling diamonds as souvenirs. The Chief could hardly take his eyes off Arxigeena all night.

Eventually, Theotokis stretched and yawned. "As much fun as this has been, we've been away three whole days now — it's time we headed home."

"Of course, of course," said the Chief. "I've already set the spaceship's autopilot — it'll fly you all straight home to Earth. Aristotelis, please, don't touch a single button this time!"

"No promises," Aristotelis grinned, but everyone laughed and climbed aboard for the journey home.

"And you, Arxigeena," the Chief said softly, "allow me to fly you home myself, in my very own ship."

Arxigeena smiled. "I would like that very much, Chief."

As the boys' spaceship rose into the night sky, waving goodbye through the windows, the Chief and Arxigeena climbed into his smaller ship and flew off together, side by side, toward Planet 650.

And from that very day, the Chief visited Planet 650 more often than any planet in the galaxy — and everyone on Aphrodite began to wonder whether a very special day might be coming soon.

And they all lived happily ever after.
