The Treasure Dragon

One day, little Rilaki was playing alone at home.
"Go on, go outside and play!"
"I'm bored... Where are Theotokis and Aristotelis?"
"Theotokis and Aristotelis are on vacation."
"Then I'll just stay here."
"Go find Dakis and Akis and play with them."

Rilaki finally got tired of sitting around and went outside. He walked over to the little monkeys' house.
"Who is it?" asked Dakis, opening the door.
"It's me, Rilaki! Come out, my friend!"
"Dakis, Akis, come outside, Rilaki's here!"
"What should we do? I'm bored too."
"We're bored too, we've been stuck inside forever. Want to go exploring in the jungle?"
"Let's go!"

The three friends set off to explore the jungle. They said they wouldn't go far, but as they were walking, Rilaki suddenly fell into a hole!
"Help, help!"
"Don't worry, I'm okay! But there's something down here!"
"What is it?"
"I don't know, come see! But climb down carefully."

Dakis and Akis climbed carefully down into the hole and found... a magic door! On the door was a riddle:
"What appears during the day and hides at night? Whoever solves this riddle will open the magic door of treasure."

The three friends thought and thought.
"Animals?" The door didn't open.
"The sky?" The door didn't open.
Their curiosity kept growing and growing. What could be behind that door?
"We have to go home and tell our parents!"

They ran home and brought back Maimudakis, the monkeys' dad, and Rillas too.
"What is it that hides at night and appears during the day?"
"Bananas!" said Rillas.
"Rillas, what are you talking about?"
They thought and thought, they guessed a thousand things — the stars, the night, the day — but nothing worked.
"We have to call Theotokis!"

They called Theotokis.
"Theotokis, we found a riddle, you have to come solve it!"
"Me, a riddle?"
"Come with Aristotelis and let's figure it out!"
"Okay!"

Theotokis called Aristotelis.
"We have to go to the jungle, our friends need us!"
"Let's go!"

The two boys arrived quickly on their four-wheelers.
"Come with us quickly, before it gets dark!"
"Okay!"

They all climbed down into the hole together and looked at the riddle.
"What appears during the day and hides at night?"
Theotokis thought about it. "It's a very easy answer. But I'm scared — we don't know what's behind that door. It could be dragons, it could be enemies, it could be treasure..."
"Should we open the door and see?"
"Are you sure?"
"Yes, we're sure!"
With full confidence, Theotokis answered: "The sun!"

Suddenly there was a strange sound and the door creaked open! They stepped inside and saw an entire meadow made of gold! But the moment they got closer... a huge dragon leapt out, breathing fire!
"He's going to eat us! Quick, close the door!"
They slammed the door shut and ran.

"He was enormous!" they all said, still shaking.
"What do we do now?"
"Didn't I tell you not to open that door?"
"Yeah, but did you see how much treasure was in there?"
"Yeah, but there's a dragon in there too, he's going to eat us!"
"Maybe we could become friends with the dragon. We don't need the treasure — maybe he's just lonely."
They decided to go home and sleep on it, and come up with a plan the next day.

Theotokis thought about it all night. The dragon was enormous, breathing fire, as big as an apartment building... But the next day, he told his friends:
"We're going back to the door to try talking to him."
"Okay!"

They went back to the door and knocked. The dragon roared and breathed fire, ready to smash the door down.
"Excuse me, Mr. Dragon," said Rilaki.
"Get away from here, you're thieves, I'll eat you!"
"Excuse me, could you calm down for a moment?"
"No! Get lost, or I'll crush you all!"
"I just want to ask you something, and you can answer if you want to. Are you alone in here? Do you have a family? Friends?"
"Get lost, I'll crush you! I don't want friends, I am the guardian of the treasure!"
"Yes, but we don't want your treasure. We just want to ask you something."
"Fine, I'll answer you. But if you say anything stupid, I'm coming out to eat you all!"

"I'll ask again: are you alone in here?"
"Of course I'm alone! I am the guardian of the treasure..."
"Don't you get bored?"
The dragon fell silent. He calmed down a little.
"Yes, I do get bored... but I am the guardian of the treasure."
"So what do you do all day?"
"Nothing. I eat, I sleep, I wake up, I eat, I sleep, I wake up. And if anyone comes in, I try to chase them off, because I am the guardian of the treasure."

"Do you want to be friends?"
"Get lost! You probably just want to come in and steal my treasure! Get out of here now, I'll eat you!"
"Do you want to be friends? Let's try an experiment. We'll come inside, and if we try to steal the treasure, you have every right to eat us. If we have a great time and then try to steal the treasure, you can still eat us. But if we have a great time and leave as friends... then we'll really be friends."

The dragon thought about it. "How many of you are there?"
"Theotokis, Aristotelis, Maimudakis, Dakis, Akis, Rillas, and Rilaki..."
"Seven!" the dragon counted. "Alright, deal. But you'll come inside, and I'll close the door myself. And if I'm not convinced we're having a good time, you won't come back out."
"Okay!"

Theotokis shouted the answer one more time: "The answer is the sun!" And the door opened.

They went inside and saw the dragon up close again. He was frightening, but his heart had already started to soften.
"So how would you like us to become friends?"
"Let's play a game! Make us mountains out of coins, and we'll turn them into slides and skateboard ramps!"
"Great idea!" said the dragon.
They played all day. At the end, the dragon asked, "Do you want to climb on my back? I'll take you all for a ride through the sky."
"Yes, yes!!"
They had the most amazing time.

They all laughed together. The dragon hadn't had this much fun in a very long time. Suddenly, they saw him start to cry.
"Why are you crying, my friend?" Rilaki asked.
"I thought that as the guardian of the treasure, I would always be alone. But you all came, and I realized how nice it is to have friends. I'm just afraid you'll lie to me and never come back."
"Don't worry. From now on, we'll be friends."
"I trust you," said the dragon.

He opened the door for them.
"When will you come back?"
"We'll come very often! Sometimes all of us, sometimes just a few, but we'll play."
"And if you ever want something to eat, a banana or two, I'll give you some."

They all went outside. They hadn't stolen a thing.
"Because I trust you and I'm grateful," said the dragon, "take these three gold coins, to buy a million bananas."
"Thank you, thank you!"
The door closed automatically behind them.

The dragon told them one last thing: "Every time you come back, you'll have to solve a riddle. Unfortunately, that's just how the magic works — it will never be the same riddle twice."
"Okay!"

And so they became friends. They went to visit him very often. Theotokis always had to be there to solve the riddles, but he didn't mind at all — because he had become friends with the dragon too. Everyone went home happy, and they bought a million bananas.

The End!
