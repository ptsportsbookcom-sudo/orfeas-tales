The Lost Little Elephant

One day, Theotokis and Aristotelis were hanging around at home, bored.
"Should we go find Rillas and the others in the jungle?" said one.
"No, let's go surprise him instead!"
"A surprise! Okay, let's go!"

They arrived at Rillas's house.
"Rillas! Are you home?"
Nothing.
"Rillas, are you home?"
Still nothing.
"Let's try Maimudakis's house instead," said Aristotelis.
"Theotokis, do you know the way?"
"Yes, I do."
"Are you sure? Last time we got completely lost."

They set off on their quad bikes, talking the whole way — and before they knew it, they were lost again. Then they heard something.
"Help! Help!"
They stopped and looked around. Nothing. They looked left, right, up, down...
Then they saw it — a huge hole in the ground, at least ten meters deep.
They walked over carefully.
"Help!"
"Hello? Is someone down there?" called Theotokis.
"Hi, I'm Ntakis the little elephant! I got separated from my mom and fell in here, and I don't know how to get out!"
"Are you okay? Does it hurt?" asked Aristotelis.
"I'm okay, but I don't know how to get out," said Ntakis, sounding scared.
"Listen," Aristotelis told Theotokis, "you stay here and keep Ntakis company — his name is Ntakis, not Takis — and I'll go find our friends and his mom."
"Okay!"

Aristotelis rode off, this way and that, getting more lost by the minute. Then he spotted a whole herd of elephants and went over to them.
"Excuse me," he said, "do you happen to know a little elephant named Ntakis?"
"We do! We've been looking everywhere for him — his mother is right behind us. Do you know where he is?"
"Yes — he fell into a big hole!"
"Oh no!" said the leader of the herd, a wise elephant named Elephanteas. "We have to help. Can you take us to him?"
"Yes, follow me!"

But between the talking and the walking, they got lost too. "Give me a minute," Aristotelis said, and called Theotokis.
"I found the elephants and his mom, but now I'm lost again!"
"Tell me what you see around you," said Theotokis.
"I — I don't know, hold on."
So Aristotelis called Rillas instead.
"Rillas! It's me. We're here with Maimudakis's family and a whole herd of elephants, looking for you. Where are you?"
"I don't know," said Rillas. "I'm lost too!"

Luckily, Rillas was the very best tracker there ever was — he could find anyone, anywhere. Once Aristotelis described where he was, Rillas set off at once with Maimudakis and the rest of the group close behind.

"There — I see the elephants!" said Rillas. Ntakis the little elephant was still down in the hole, with Theotokis keeping him company and waiting for help. The whole herd had arrived by now, including Ntakis's mother, Ntina the elephant.
"Ntakis, Ntakis, are you all right?" she called down.
"Yes, Mom, but I'm scared I won't be able to get out!"
"I have a plan," said Theotokis, "but we're going to need some strength."
"We're elephants," said the herd. "Strength is our specialty!"
"See those two big trees? We need to push them down into the hole with your trunks — carefully — so Rillas can climb down and lift Ntakis out."
"Ntakis weighs a hundred kilos!" said Rillas. "You want me to carry him?"
"No worries — you can do it!"

Ten elephants leaned into the first tree, and ten more into the second, easing both trunks down into the hole slowly, so as not to hit Ntakis. Rillas climbed down to try to reach him — but the trunks were too smooth and slippery to climb back up.
"We have a problem, guys — it's too slippery!"
"No problem," said Aristotelis. "I have an idea!"
He gathered up long jungle vines and wound them round and round the fallen trunks, in and out, until they formed a proper rope ladder.
Rillas climbed down, lifted Ntakis carefully onto his back, and carried him — step by careful step — all the way up and out of the hole.
"Oh, thank you so much!" said Ntina, hugging her son. "I don't know what we would have done without you all."
"It was nothing!" said Rillas.
"Is there anything we can do to thank you?"
"Not a thing — we're just glad he's safe."

Everyone said their goodbyes and headed home.
"What a day," said Rillas with a yawn. "Thank you both for finding him."
"Anytime," said Theotokis and Aristotelis.
As they walked off, Aristotelis grinned. "You basically had Ntakis riding on your back the whole way."
"He was heavy," laughed Rillas. "Goodnight, everyone — goodnight!"

The End!
