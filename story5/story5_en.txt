One day, Theotokis and Aristotelis decided to visit their friend, Rillas the gorilla. They jumped on their quad bikes and roared off into the jungle.

When they arrived at Rillas's home, they found him sitting outside with his head hung low. Something was very wrong.

"What's the matter, Rillas? Are you okay?" asked Aristotelis.

"I'm not okay, boys," said Rillas with a heavy sigh. "My cousin, Gorillatsas, disappeared a whole month ago. I searched everywhere in the jungle, but I found nothing."

"A whole month?!" gasped Theotokis. "Aristotelis, we have to help!"

"Right away!" said Aristotelis. "Show us his home, Rillas. Maybe we'll find a clue."

They went inside Gorillatsas's house. As soon as they opened the door, the sight left them speechless: the door had been smashed and everything was thrown around in a mess. Someone had forced their way in.

"This didn't happen by itself," said Theotokis, thinking hard. "Someone took him. I suspect the Poachers."

"Poachers?" said Aristotelis.

"Poachers," explained Theotokis, "are bad people who capture rare animals to sell them. And a little gorilla is a very rare animal indeed."

They searched carefully around the outside of the house. Aristotelis dropped to the ground and looked closely at the dirt. "Tyre tracks!" he called out. "The jungle preserved them! Let's follow them!"

They left their quad bikes behind — the noise would give them away — and started walking. They walked for three whole days through the jungle, following the tracks.

It was exhausting. "Three days of walking," grumbled Aristotelis. "Oh, where are my quad bikes now..."

Suddenly, Theotokis raised his hand. "Stop! I see something ahead."

Through the trees, they made out a large building. It was the Poachers' hideout.

"Gorillatsas must be in there," whispered Rillas.

"We can't just walk in," said Theotokis. "Look — they have defences. Archers on the walls!"

"What do we do now?" asked Aristotelis.

Theotokis thought for a moment. "At home, I have an old suit of armour — the kind knights used to wear. No arrow can get through it. If someone puts on the armour and carries a huge shield, they can draw the guards' attention. Meanwhile, we slip inside."

"I'll do the distraction!" said Rillas. "I'm the strongest. And he's my cousin."

They went back home, spent a week preparing, and returned ready for battle.

Rillas put on the armour — a shiny, spectacular suit with an enormous shield. He looked like a gorilla-knight!

Rillas stepped forward. The archers fired arrow after arrow, but every single one bounced off the shield with a loud clang. Rillas kept moving forward steadily, and the guards began to panic.

At that moment, Theotokis and Aristotelis slipped inside like ninjas.

Inside, they found three cages. "Oh my goodness," whispered Aristotelis.

"Gorillatsas!" called Theotokis softly.

A voice came from the first cage. "Here! I'm in here!"

"We found him! But the keys..."

The keys were in another room. They crept in like ninjas, grabbed the keys, and opened all three cages.

Inside was Gorillatsas — Rillas's cousin. And with him, a little monkey called Maimudakis, and a female gorilla named Rilena.

"Freedom!" shouted Gorillatsas.

At that very moment, alarms blared. "Alert! All the animals have escaped!"

Rillas, the moment he heard his cousin's voice, burst through the walls. He smashed everything in his path. The Poachers fled in every direction, terrified.

Running together, they grabbed their quad bikes and disappeared into the jungle. They were free!

Deep in the jungle, Gorillatsas ran to hug his uncle. "Thank you, cousin Rillas. I didn't know if I was going to make it."

"Thank you too, Rilena," said Aristotelis.

"And you, Maimudakis," said Theotokis. "Where do you live?"

"Nearby," said Maimudakis. "I'll head home now."

"Do you want to be friends?" asked Rillas.

"Yes, yes, yes!"

Rillas turned to Rilena. "And you? Where do you live?" His ears had gone red. He liked Rilena very much.

"Nearby too," said Rilena with a smile.

"Would you... would you all like to be friends?" said Rillas, all in a rush.

Everyone laughed. "Yes — having friends is very important," said Theotokis.

And so the group grew even bigger. Theotokis shook hands with Aristotelis. "We did it again, friend."

"We always do," said Aristotelis with his big grin. "Now — where are my quad bikes?"
