Saving the Dragon

Once upon a time, little Rilaki was sitting at home with nothing to do.

"I'm so bored," he sighed. "I know! I'll go and find my friends Dakis and Akis, and we'll go and visit our friend the Dragon. We haven't seen him in such a long time. Let's see how he's doing!"

So Rilaki went over to the little monkeys' house.

"Hey guys, what are you up to? We're so bored at home!"

"Oh my goodness, I've got a brilliant idea," said Rilaki. "Shall we go and find our friend the Dragon?"

"Yes, yes, yes!" said Dakis. "Let's go! It's been ages since we saw him. He must be so bored."

"Okay, okay!"

But Rilaki had forgotten something. To open the Dragon's magic door, you have to solve a riddle. And every time, it's a different riddle.

Anyway, off they went, happy as can be, and soon they found the door.

"Oh my goodness," said Rilaki. "Okay. I hope the riddle is an easy one."

The riddle on the door said:
"When you say my name, I disappear. What am I?"

"Oh my goodness," said Rilaki. "Dakis, do you know?"

"How would I know? This thing is really, really hard!"

"Akis, what do you think?"

"No idea. What do we do now?"

"Let's call Theotokis!" said Dakis.

"Wait... oh my goodness, I left the phone at home!"

"Okay, let's go home, ask Theotokis the riddle, and then come back and solve it."

"Okay, okay!"

At home they found Rillas, Rilaki's dad.

"What's up, little Rillas? What's going on?"

"Dad, we're going to visit our friend the Dragon, but I forgot about the riddle, and we need to call Theotokis."

"Ah, yes, yes! Here, take my phone."

Ring ring... ring ring...

"Hello?"

"Theotokis, it's me, Rilaki! How are you guys? All good?"

"All good! What's up?"

"We're going to see our friend the Dragon, and there's a riddle."

"Oh my goodness, I love riddles!" said Theotokis. "Tell me!"

"When you say my name, I disappear. What am I?"

"That's super easy, guys, super easy! The answer is silence. Because the moment you say 'silence', it isn't silent any more!"

So the three friends went back to the door.

"That really was super easy!" they laughed. And all together they shouted: "SILENCE!"

The door creaked open, and in they went.

"Dragon! Dragon, are you here?"

No answer.

"Dragon! Dragon, are you here?"

Nothing.

Behind the door was a whole world: a huge kingdom with its own forests, hills and lakes. But the Dragon was nowhere to be seen, and his treasure was lying there with no one guarding it. And here and there, wandering about... there were lions!

"Oh my goodness," said Rilaki. "What kind of trouble have we got ourselves into?"

"I don't know what to do," said Dakis. "Quick, quick, we have to go back and call Theotokis again. The boys have to come, and Dad too!"

"Okay, okay!"

So they ran home again, and Rilaki called Theotokis and Aristotelis.

"Guys, our friend the Dragon is missing!"

"Our friend the Dragon? Let's go, right now!"

The boys jumped on their quad bikes and raced to the jungle. This time everyone came: Rillas, Maimudakis, Dakis, Akis, Rilaki, Theotokis and Aristotelis.

"Oh no," said Rilaki at the door. "I forgot. There's a new riddle every time!"

And this riddle was much harder:
"You are the bus driver. Ten people get on the bus. At the next stop, five get off. Then ten get on. Then three more. Then thirteen more. How old is the bus driver?"

"Wait," said Rilaki. "Who is the bus driver? Some monster?"

"No!" said Theotokis. "Read it again. It says YOU are the bus driver. So how old is the bus driver?"

Rilaki's face lit up. "Me? I'm almost six!"

"Bravo!" And the door swung wide open.

They all went inside, calling: "Dragon! Dragon!"

Theotokis bent down and studied the ground. "Guys, come and look at this. These marks... something big was dragging itself along here."

"We have to follow the tracks."

A little further on, Theotokis found a broken branch. "Someone came through here." A little further: "I see footprints. Someone definitely came this way." And then, another broken branch.

"Oh my goodness, this is very mysterious," said Theotokis. "Where is our friend the Dragon? Did someone attack him?"

"Come on, he's a dragon!" said Aristotelis. "Who would attack a dragon? He'd turn them to ash!"

"I don't know. Let's go and see."

A little further on, they found ash on the ground.

And then they found him: their friend the Dragon, lying beside a lake, unconscious, with a hurt wing.

"Oh my goodness! Friend, friend, are you okay?"

The Dragon slowly opened his eyes. "Oh... thank you for coming. I'm not feeling very well."

"What happened, friend?"

"I went out for a little flight. I was doing dives over the lake, whoosh, whoosh! But on one dive, I tore my wing on a pine tree. It hurt so much that a burst of fire came out of me. Then I fell into the lake, and little by little, I fainted."

"We were so worried! We thought someone had attacked you!"

"What do we do now?"

"I know," said Theotokis. "First we put a little alcohol on it to clean it. Then I'll stitch it up. I know how, I'm very good at it. And then we'll bandage it."

"But where do we take him? There's nobody here to help."

"We'll make a stretcher and carry him all together, slowly, slowly."

"To his home, where his gold is. That's where he'll get better."

"Okay, okay!"

It was a very hard mission. They built a huge stretcher. Theotokis cleaned and bandaged the wound. The Dragon's home was far away, but our friends were strong and determined.

"When I say 'hup'," said Theotokis, "we all take one step. Okay? Slowly, slowly."

They lifted the Dragon onto the stretcher.

"Heave... hup! Heave... hup! Heave... hup!"

Step by step, they carried him all the way home.

"What will he eat now? What does a dragon even eat?"

"I eat special food," the Dragon told them. "But the way I am now, I can't go far to get it."

"Okay," said Aristotelis. "Theotokis and I will go and get the food. Rillas and the others will bring him water, lots and lots of water."

"Okay, okay!"

Bringing water was an easy mission for Rillas and the others. But Theotokis and Aristotelis had to climb the highest mountain in the Dragon's kingdom. Luckily, the Dragon told them about a secret path that only he knew, so the climb would be easier. Even so, it was a whole hour of walking, up and up and up. At the top they gathered lots of food and carried it all the way back.

"Here you go. Now get better soon!"

"Thank you, guys, a thousand thanks," said the Dragon. "I don't know what would have happened to me if you hadn't come. You saved my life. I will be grateful to you forever."

"We'll come and see you again next week," said Rilaki. "We'll come often, we promise. Sorry it took us so long to visit this time."

"No, no, I understand," said the Dragon. "You are my friends. You didn't lie to me. You came back. You are true friends. Thank you."

"Friends forever. Okay, okay!"

You see, the Dragon can never leave his kingdom. He has to stay inside forever, guarding the treasure. There is a magic on the door that nobody can break: visitors can come in, but the Dragon can never go out, because then no one would be guarding the treasure. One day, someone will have to break that magic. Maybe, in the future, Theotokis will find a way.

And so our friends went home, each to his own house, happy that they had saved their friend.

The End
