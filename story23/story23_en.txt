The Race for Her Heart

Ever since he flew Arxigeena home to Planet 650, the Chief could not stop thinking about her. He tossed and turned every night, waking early and staying up late, going over the same question again and again: how could he ask her on a proper date?

"What if I ask and she says no?" he muttered, pacing around his spaceship. "And if she says yes, where would I even take her? What would I wear? Which ship would I fly?"

Finally, he grabbed his walkie-talkie and called his old friend Rillas. "Rillas, do you copy? Chief here, over."

"Rillas here, loud and clear! What's going on, Chief?"

"I need some advice, man. I want to ask a certain someone out, but I don't want to mess it up."

Rillas laughed so hard he nearly dropped the walkie-talkie. In the end all he said was, "Just call her, Chief! What are you waiting for?"

Still nervous, the Chief called Theotokis and Aristotelis too, hoping for better advice. "Great idea!" said Theotokis. "Call her tonight!"

"Yeah, but what if she says no?" the Chief fretted.

"Relax," said Aristotelis. "Stay calm, don't overthink it, and see what happens."

So the Chief stood in front of his communicator for days, working up the courage to press the call button. Finally, one evening, he did.

"Hello? Is this... Arxigeena?"

"Chief! How are you? I was actually just thinking about you."

His heart leapt. "Would you like to do something together tomorrow?"

"I would love that. Yes, yes, yes!"

"What do you like to do for fun?"

"I love spaceship races — the ones on Planet 67!"

"Then that's exactly where we'll go."

The next morning, the Chief gathered his credits and bought two tickets to the biggest spaceship race in the galaxy — thirty of the best pilots in the universe, racing across a track packed with fire rings, moving walls, sharp turns, and a terrifying twenty-metre jump.

He and Arxigeena arrived early, grabbing popcorn and drinks before finding their seats. While they waited, Arxigeena turned to him with a grin. "Want to make a friendly bet? We each pick a racer. If my pilot wins, you're taking me to dinner. If yours wins... I owe you a big hug."

"Deal," the Chief laughed.

Arxigeena studied the lineup and pointed to a small, plain little ship near the back that nobody else seemed to notice. "I'm picking number one."

"That one? Not the big shiny one at the front?"

"Nope. Number one."

"Then I'll take twenty."

"Ladies and gentlemen," boomed the announcer, "welcome to the greatest spaceship race on Planet 67! Thirty of the best pilots in the universe, on the deadliest track ever built — fire, moving walls, sudden drops, everything! Three, two, one... go!"

Ship twenty rocketed into an early lead, while Arxigeena's tiny ship number one crawled along near the back. At the first turn, half the fleet spun out, leaving twenty ships. Through the Ring of Fire, ten more were knocked out. Past the spike field, only eight remained — twenty still in front, one still dead last. Then came the giant twenty-metre jump, and three more crashed, leaving just five.

"Incredible!" cried the announcer. "Number twenty has led from the start — but on a track this dangerous, anything can happen!"

Lap after lap, ship after ship wiped out, until only two were left: twenty and one. "Five laps to go, and twenty still leads by ten seconds!" Then, "Wait — what's this? Number one is closing the gap! Down to just one second, and taking the corners tight!"

The Chief gripped his seat. "Oh no. Don't tell me your little underdog is actually going to catch up."

They reached the final stretch — the most dangerous part of the whole track, packed with fire traps and crumbling walls. Ship twenty, far too bulky, got stuck trying to squeeze through the narrow gap in the flames. But ship one, tiny and nimble, slipped straight through without a scratch — and crossed the finish line first.

Arxigeena burst out laughing, delighted. "Ha! I told you — number one!"

"You did," the Chief admitted with a grin, "which means I owe you dinner — and I'm hoping it turns into a few more dinners after that."

"I was hoping the same thing," Arxigeena said softly. "I had a wonderful time with you today."

That night, the Chief walked her home himself. And as they said goodnight, both of them knew that this was only the beginning of something wonderful.
