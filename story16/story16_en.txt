Rilaki's Lesson

Once upon a time, Theotokis and Aristotelis had a friend, Rillas, who had a son, Rilaki, and a friend, Maimudakis, whose sons were Dakis and Akis. Rilaki, Dakis, and Akis were the best of friends. They played together, did everything together, got excited about every adventure, went on outings, and played hide-and-seek for hours.

One day, though, Rilaki's behavior started to change. Instead of playing with his friends, he began hitting them, shouting at them, and telling them: "If you don't do what I want, I won't play with you."

For a while, Dakis and Akis just went along with whatever Rilaki wanted. One day their dad noticed something was off. "Why aren't you two going to play with Rilaki?" Maimudakis asked. "We're a little sad and confused," they told him. "We used to have such a good time with Rilaki, but lately he's been mean — he yells at us, he hits us." "Did you tell him you don't like it?" "No, we never said anything." "Well, how's he supposed to know? Why don't you try telling him?" "Okay, okay."

The next day, while they were playing nicely, Rilaki started again: "You have to do what I want — you don't know better, only I do." Dakis spoke up: "Hey, are you okay?" "I'm fine," said Rilaki. "Why?" "Because lately you've been a bad friend." "What do you mean? We've always played like this." "No," said Dakis, "before, you listened to what we wanted too, and we all did things together. Now you only want us to do what you do, and you hit us too." "Really?" Rilaki asked. "Akis, do you agree?" "Yes," said Akis, "exactly. We don't know what changed, but you've become a bad friend. And if you don't change, we don't want to play with you anymore." Rilaki got upset. "Fine, I don't care — I'm going home."

He went home and told himself he didn't mind at all. "Good for me," he thought, playing alone with his toys. "I don't need friends. I don't care."

One day passed, then two, and on the third day he got bored of playing alone. "I'll go find my friends," he said. He knocked on the door, and Maimudakis answered. "Hey, Rilaki, how's it going?" "Are Dakis and Akis here? I haven't seen them in days." "I don't know... maybe they're inside. Let me go call them."

Maimudakis went in. "Dakis, Akis, come on out — Rilaki's here to play." "Tell him to go away," they said. "Why?" "Because we already told him he's not being a good friend, and we told him if he doesn't care about us, he can stay by himself. So now we want him to know that we don't care either — he clearly doesn't want friends. If he'd rather be alone all day than hit us and bother us, let him be alone." Maimudakis went back to Rilaki. "They're not coming, Rilaki." "Why not? Can't they come?" "They told me they don't want to play with you anymore — that you're not a good friend, you're a bad friend." "But why? I'm doing the same things we've always done." "And what were those things?" Maimudakis asked. "We played whatever I wanted." "And you think that's how it should be?" "Of course. I'm the one who knows — they don't." "Then they're not coming, buddy. Why don't you go home and think about what happened, try to understand it, and we'll see." "Okay, okay."

He went home. "I don't care, I'll just play by myself," he said. A day went by, a week went by, a month went by — nobody came looking for him. He was completely alone, and slowly he grew sad.

Rillas noticed. "What's wrong, Rilaki? What's going on?" "I'm sad," he said. "I don't have any friends." "Why not?" "Because nobody wants to play with me." "But how did that happen?" Rillas asked. "You've got Dakis and Akis, you play together all day. Did something happen?" "No, nothing happened. They just don't want to play with me anymore." "Okay, okay," said Rillas. "Let me call Maimudakis."

He called Aristotelis and Theotokis. "Do you want to come with me over to Maimudakis's place, to see what's going on with Rilaki?" "We're coming right now!" They hopped on their quad bikes — vroom, vroom, vroom, vroom! — picked up Rillas along the way, and headed over together.

"What's going on, buddy?" Rillas asked Maimudakis. "Rilaki told me your boys don't want to play with him anymore." "Let me tell you," said Maimudakis. "Rilaki started acting up — bothering them, hitting them, always telling them what to do. So I told the boys to speak up and tell him they don't like it. They did, and they told him his behavior wasn't right, that he wasn't being a good friend. And he told them: 'I don't care if you don't understand — I'd rather be alone.'" "Seriously?" Rillas asked, stunned. "Seriously." "Good heavens!" said Theotokis. "My friend Rilaki did all that? Why?" "I don't know," said Rillas. "Maybe he thinks it's fine to do whatever he wants and not have friends." "Let's go talk to him properly," said Theotokis.

They went to find Rilaki. "Hey, Rilaki," Theotokis said, "are you okay?" "No," he said. "I'm not okay." "Why not?" "Because my friends are ignoring me, nobody wants to play with me, and I don't understand why." "Are you a good friend?" Theotokis asked. "Of course — the best! They did whatever I wanted. Sure, I hit them sometimes, but only because they didn't understand... I'm the best friend there is." "Think back to the last time you played something your friends wanted," said Theotokis. "Never," said Rilaki. "Why would I do something I don't want to do?" "When was the last time Dakis asked you not to hit him, and you actually listened?" "Never. Why shouldn't I hit him, if he doesn't get it?" "And did you do the same to Akis?" "Yes, exactly the same." "So how do you feel now, Mr. Rilaki, that they don't want to play with you?" "Sad. I have no friends, and I did everything for them." "Think about everything you just told me," said Theotokis, "and tell me honestly — were you a good friend or a bad one? Did you make them do only what you wanted?" "Yes." "So you never played what they wanted — is that what good friends do?" "No." "Did you hit them?" "I hit them." "Did you yell at them?" "I yelled at them." "And when they didn't do what you wanted, what did you do?" "I left." "Let me ask you something else. If Dakis constantly told you what to do, would you like it?" "No." "If Akis hit you, would you like it?" "No." "And if Dakis and Akis played with other kids and left you out, would you like it?" "No." "So what did you do when they played with other kids?" "I got mad at them. Why won't everyone just play with me?" "Okay. Think about everything we just talked about. What do you think — are you a good friend, or a bad one?"

Rilaki thought about it for a long time. "If someone did this to me, I wouldn't like it at all," he thought. "I like getting my way, but that means I've been a bad friend. Or maybe it means I've been a little selfish." He thought and thought — and at some point, he started to cry.

"Why are you crying?" Rillas asked. "I'm crying because I really was a bad friend," said Rilaki. "I wasn't a good friend at all. I always wanted things my way, I hit my friends, I didn't listen when Mom and Dad tried to tell me. I was a very bad friend, and that's why I ended up alone. Neither Dakis nor Akis want anything to do with me. My behavior wasn't right, and they told me so — they warned me, and I didn't listen. And now I'm alone. That's why I'm crying." "Do you think there's something you can do to change things?" Theotokis asked. "I don't know... I want to go apologize to them and tell them that from now on I'll be a good, cooperative friend. But I'm scared — what if they don't accept it?" "Are they even talking to you right now?" "No." "So what do you have to lose? Either they forgive you, or things stay exactly like they are now." "You're right," said Rilaki. "Let's go."

They went to Maimudakis's house. "Dakis, Akis, come outside — Rilaki wants to tell you something." "We don't want to talk to him, not after..." "Come on out, boys." They stepped outside. "What do you want to tell us, Rilaki?" "I want to say I'm sorry," said Rilaki. "I thought about it — Theotokis and my dad both talked to me — and it's true, I was a bad friend. Instead of playing together and doing things we all wanted, it was always just about me, and I hit you too. I want to apologize." "You can apologize, but are you going to do the same things again?" Dakis and Akis asked. "Because if you do, we don't want to be your friends." "No, no," Rilaki promised. "I'll change. I'll be more cooperative, I won't hit anyone. And if there's something I don't feel like doing, I'll just leave you be. If you want to play with other kids and I don't feel like it, that's fine — I'll wait patiently, or I'll go play with my other friends. I promise I'll never tell you what to do again. I'll let you do what you want, because we're friends, and we'll play the things all three of us want to play. Deal?" "Deal!"

And from that day on, Rilaki went from being a bad friend to being the best friend anyone could ask for. Because now he listened to his friends, he never bothered them again, and they listened right back to him too. And the three of them played together, just the way friends should.
