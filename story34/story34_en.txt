The Banana Thief

Once upon a time, Rillas and Rilaki woke up one morning in their treehouse, and all their bananas were gone! There were strange little footprints on the floor, and a small window was wide open.

"Oh my goodness!" cried Rillas. "What happened? We've been robbed!"

"And our food! What are we going to do now?"

"I know," said Rilaki. "Why don't we call Theotokis and Aristotelis? They're detectives. They'll help us find out what happened!"

"Very good idea!"

So Rillas phoned Theotokis. Ring ring... ring ring...

"Hey, Rillas! What's up, friend?"

"Oh, Theotokis, I need your help! I'm so worried! Somebody stole all my bananas! I saw some footprints, and now we're hungry!"

"What? They stole your bananas? Don't worry, we're on our way!"

Theotokis called Aristotelis right away. Beep, beep, beep... "Aristotelis! Somebody robbed Rillas! Get ready, I'm coming to get you!"

"Okay!"

The boys jumped on their quad bikes. Brrrm, brrrm... and off they raced to the jungle.

Theotokis brought his folders and his notepad to write down clues. As soon as they arrived, he said, "Everyone out! This is a crime scene!"

Everyone went outside. "Now, Rillas, show me slowly what happened."

Rillas showed him the banana peels on the floor and the footprints. He leaned in closer and closer... "Back, gorilla!" said Theotokis. "Back!"

Theotokis took out his magnifying glass and studied the footprints. "Oh my! These are the footprints... of a monkey!"

"How did you figure that out?" asked Rillas.

"Oh, Rillas, please, please, please!" said Theotokis. "We'll follow the clues and the tracks."

"But what tracks?" asked Rillas. "Didn't I tell you the window was open? So... so... the window was open."

"Rillas... shhh! Focus, please! The window was open, so... the thief came in through the window! We'll follow his steps backwards, and they will lead us to him."

"Okay, okay!"

So they climbed out of the window to look for more clues. Tsup! Two more footprints on the branch outside!

"Aristotelis, they're the same!" said Theotokis. "Follow me!"

They climbed down from the treehouse and looked here and there. Then they found a little tree, a bit broken.

"The thief came through here, one hundred percent!" said Theotokis. "Let's go, we have lots of clues!"

Theotokis bent lower and lower with his magnifying glass, just like Sherlock Holmes. Soon they found more footprints. "He came this way! This way!"

They walked and walked, until the jungle became so thick that they couldn't go any further. There were trees everywhere and leaves everywhere.

"Hmm," said Theotokis. "If I were a little monkey, what would I do?"

"You'd climb the tree!" said Aristotelis.

"Bravo, Aristotelis! Rillas, Rilaki, climb the trees and look for clues!"

"But where do I put my feet? Do I have to jump?" grumbled Rillas.

"Just find a footprint, or something broken, so we know where this little monkey went!"

(And no, Orfeas, it wasn't our friend Maimudakis! It was a monkey nobody had ever met before. You'll see.)

So Rillas and Rilaki climbed the trees and looked here and there, until Rilaki found a little tuft of monkey fur.

"Oh my! He must have passed through here," said Theotokis. "And he must be going that way."

"How do you know that, Theotokis?"

"I've read all the Sherlock Holmes books! We follow the clues!"

"Okay!"

They followed the trail for many, many hours. A few footprints here, a few hairs there... Until at last they reached a huge tree. And high up at the top, there was a nest.

"The thief must be here," said Theotokis.

"How are you so sure?"

"He must be here."

"So what do we do now? We can't just climb up. They might attack us!"

"I have an idea," said Theotokis. "We'll hide here and throw stones at the tree to see what happens."

"Okay, okay!"

They threw the first stone. Clang! Nothing moved. They threw the second stone. Clang! A whole flock of birds flew out. Tweet, tweet, tweet, tweet!

Then a monkey came out. "What's going on here?" He looked a little sick, tired and worn out.

He climbed down, and everyone jumped out of their hiding place. "You're the thief!"

"No, no! I didn't steal anything!"

"Confess! You're the thief!" said Theotokis. "I can see some tufts of fur missing from your head. You must have scraped it on the branches!"

"No, no, no!"

"We're going up to your house right now."

"Oh, please don't go up there! I beg you, leave us alone! No, no!"

"We're going," said Theotokis. "You're the thief!"

So they climbed up, Orfeas, and what did they find? A tiny baby monkey... and Rillas's bananas!

"Guilty! Guilty!" they shouted. "We'll take him to prison!"

"But we're in the jungle," said Rilaki. "There's no prison here!"

"Hmm... then I'll be the judge!" said Aristotelis. "And we'll put him on trial!"

So they all sat down in the shade under the tree.

"Why did you steal Rillas's bananas instead of finding your own?" asked the judge.

The baby monkey started to cry.

The father monkey knew he couldn't hide the truth any longer. "I am the thief," he said sadly. "I'm sorry. But for a whole week I've been searching here and searching there, and I couldn't find any food for my hungry baby. We had nothing left, and I didn't know what to do. Then, as I was passing by, I saw Mr. Rillas's window open, and I went inside. I'm truly sorry. Take the bananas back, and take me to the animal prison... even though there isn't one."

"But why didn't you ask for help?"

"I have no one to ask. We're all alone here. Look around, is there anyone else?"

"Well, Rillas," said the judge, "what punishment should we give this monkey?"

Rillas had listened to the whole story, Orfeas, and he was very moved.

"Nothing," he said.

"What?!" said the judge. "Why?"

"You heard his story, boys. If Rilaki and I had been in the same trouble, I would have done the same thing. Stealing is not right, but this father had no other choice."

Then Rillas turned to the monkey. "Listen. I'll bring you bananas once a week. We have lots of them over at our place. But never steal again!"

"Oh, thank you!" said the father monkey. "I'm so sorry. What can I do for you in return?"

"Well, now that you mention it," said Rillas, "we do have some chores at the treehouse..."

"Of course! My little one and I will come and help you!"

"Bravo, Rillas," said Theotokis. "You were very kind. The kindest of all!"

And they all lived happily ever after... and we lived even better!
