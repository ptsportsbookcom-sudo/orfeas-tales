The Wedding of Rillas and Rilena

One day, after spending a lot of time with Rilena, Rillas decided it was time — he had to ask her to marry him. But he was so anxious about it. "Oh no," he said to himself, "what if she says no? What if she'd rather go out with her friends? I don't know what to do... maybe I should go ask the guys what they think."

Completely by coincidence, Theotokis and Aristotelis had decided that same day to go into the jungle to find Rillas and see what he was up to. They set off, reached the jungle, and found him there.

"Hey Rillas, what's up man?" "Ah, leave it, guys." "Come on, what's wrong?" asked Aristotelis. "I don't know what to do." "Did you get hurt?" "No, no, I'm fine... it's about Rilena." "So what's the problem?" "I want to ask her to marry me." "Okay!" "But what if she says no?" "You'll never know if you don't ask her..." "Yes, but I want to do something really impressive, you know?"

That's when Theotokis had an idea. "Did you know I can marry you two myself? I did an online course on the internet — I'm allowed to officiate weddings now! Want us to surprise her?" "But what if she says no?" "Let her say no then! Just tell her how you feel." "Okay... okay, let's do the surprise."

"Here's the plan," said Theotokis. "Tonight we'll all help you. You take her to the lake — your favorite spot — and propose there. Then we'll bring all your friends to surprise you both. If she says yes, we'll come out and celebrate. If she says no... well, you just won't call us over." "Okay, okay. But it has to stay a secret — you can't tell her anything!" "We'll manage it. Should we go tell Maimoudakis and Gorillatsos too?"

"Are those really your friends?" Rillas laughed. "And what, you two aren't enough? I've got three friends, that's plenty!" "Okay, okay," they laughed back. "Wait, isn't Gorillatsos... your cousin?" "Yeah, my cousin — but he's my friend too, obviously. Anyway, go tell them!"

Theotokis and Aristotelis went and found Maimoudakis first. "Rillas wants to propose to Rilena — we're going to surprise them, are you in?" "Of course I'm in! Let him ask her already, she'll say yes for sure!" Then they found Gorillatsos and told him the plan too. "Perfect! I know she'll say yes, everything's going to go great!" Both were thrilled and kept asking, "So when is Rillas actually going to tell her?"

But a week went by. Then two weeks. Then three, then four — and Rillas still hadn't said anything. "I think he's getting cold feet," Aristotelis told Theotokis. "Let's go find him and see what's going on." They went back to the jungle. "So, what's happening?" they asked. "I'll tell you what's happening," said Rillas. "I'm sure she's going to say no."

"Come on, Rillas, just ask her whatever you want to ask her!" "Should we go tell her ourselves?" "No, no! You'll ruin the surprise!" "Fine — next week, then. It's settled, you're proposing next week." "Perfect, perfect." "Okay — we'll go get the party ready on Sunday. If she says no, whistle to us like this — 'ssss' — so we know not to come out. If she says yes, bring her to the spot after a little while and we'll be waiting."

Monday went by, Tuesday went by, Rillas was a nervous wreck. Wednesday, Thursday, Friday came and went. Finally, on Saturday, he asked Rilena: "Hey, Rilena, want to do something on Sunday? Go to our favorite spot?" "Sure, let's go!" she said. "It'll be perfect," he told her. "I'll bring bananas — you don't have to do anything, I'll take care of everything."

Sunday came. Rillas was sweating with nerves, terrified she might say no. They went to the lake — it was so beautiful there, a perfectly clear sky, a lovely day. "Should I tell her? Should I not tell her? Should I tell her..." he kept thinking. Finally he said, "Rilena..." "Yes, Rillas? Do you want to tell me something?" "I... yes..." "Come on, sweetheart, I can tell something's on your mind — just tell me, it's okay."

So he got serious, took out a ring, and gave it to her. "Rilena," he said, "will you marry me?" "Oh my goodness," she said, "you've made me so happy! Of course, yes — I love you so much. I was waiting for so long, I honestly thought you'd never ask, because we always have such a wonderful time together. Yes, let's get married!"

They sat by the lake a while longer, hugging, kissing, so very happy. "I have one more surprise for you," Rillas said. "What surprise? Come with me!" He led her into a clearing in the moonlit night.

Trees and branches had been arranged to hide the entrance, and as they stepped through, lights suddenly turned on. There stood Theotokis, Aristotelis, Maimoudakis, and Gorillatsos, all beaming with joy — with bananas, drinks, and Aristotelis on music duty as the DJ. "Did she say yes?" they shouted. "Yes! Yes! Yes!" Rillas called back.

"But now," said Rilena, "I'm not ready to get married — I haven't planned anything!" "Now?" said Theotokis. "I have the license from the internet — I'll marry you right now!" They brought her a bouquet and a white veil for her head. "You look perfect," everyone told her, "just perfect."

Rillas stood there beaming, all natural and easygoing as always. Theotokis, on the other hand, put on his most serious face, placed the golden wedding crowns on them, and began: "The servant of God, Rillas, weds the handmaid of God, Rilena. The handmaid of God, Rilena, weds the servant of God, Rillas..." He went on and on with official-sounding words, very solemn.

At some point he said, "Rillas, do you take Rilena as your wife, to love her forever and take care of her?" "I do," said Rillas. "Rilena, do you take Rillas as your husband, to take care of him forever?" There was a tense pause — "Uh oh, is she going to say no?" someone whispered nervously. Then Rilena laughed. "Of course I take him — I love him so, so much."

"Then, by the power vested in me by the internet," announced Theotokis, "I now pronounce you husband and wife. You may kiss the bride, Rillas!" Everyone cheered, and the party began — music, dancing, laughter, pure joy.

In the middle of all the celebration, someone else heard the music from far away and came closer, timidly — tsik, tsik, tsik. It was a monkey nobody recognized. "What's going on here?" she asked shyly. Rillas, thrilled as ever, welcomed her at once: "Come on in! It's a wedding party — come have a great time, eat some bananas with us!"

Maimoudakis looked at her — and fell in love on the spot. "Oh my goodness," he whispered to himself, staring, completely stunned, nearly tripping over his own feet. "Who... who is that?" It turned out her name was Maimoudakena — and from that night on, she became their newest friend. Everyone had a wonderful time, and they all lived happily ever after. The End.
