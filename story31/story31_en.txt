The Great Banana Contest

Once upon a time, Rillas was in the jungle, looking for bananas.

He walked and walked, and without noticing, he left his own part of the jungle and wandered into somebody else's.

And there, high up in a tree, he saw it: a huge bunch of bananas. "Oh my goodness! So ripe! So delicious!"

He reached up to grab them... when out jumped King Kong, the enormous black gorilla.

"ROOOAAAR! Get out of here! What do you want with my bananas?"

"Your bananas?" said Rillas. "Why, are they yours?"

"I saw them first!" said Rillas.

"No, I saw them first!" said King Kong.

"And who are you?"

"I am King Kong, the strongest gorilla in the jungle! Leave now, before I beat you and take the bananas!"

"Nonsense!" said Rillas. "You? Beat me? Off you go!"

They shouted this way, they shouted that way. Then along came a wise old gorilla called Evripidis, leaning on his walking stick.

"What's going on here, boys? Why all the shouting?"

"This one wants to take my bananas," said King Kong, "and I saw them first!"

"Well," said Old Evripidis, "I can see you're about to fight. So I propose a contest. Whoever wins gets to eat all the bananas."

"Alright, alright," said Rillas. "What do you have in mind?"

"A contest of speed, strength and brains. First, a race from tree to tree: that's for speed. Then we'll see who can lift the most: that's for strength. And last, a puzzle: that's for brains. Whoever wins the most gets the bunch."

"And how do we know," said King Kong, "that you won't eat the bananas yourself?"

"Are you serious?" said Old Evripidis.

"Yes, I'm serious."

"Fine. Then call your friends to come and watch."

"Alright, alright."

Rillas picked up his phone and called Theotokis and Aristotelis. "Boys, come to the jungle, right now!"

"What happened now?"

"Oh my goodness, something's happened to him!"

They jumped on their quad bikes, diri-diri-di-di-di, and when they arrived they honked their horns. BEEP BEEP!

Then they saw King Kong. "Oh my goodness! Him again! We're done for! Help! HELP!"

"Don't be scared," said Rillas. "We're having a contest."

King Kong had never learned to talk to humans, so Old Evripidis explained everything to the boys. And King Kong called his family and friends to come and watch too.

"Are you ready?"

"But what kind of contest is it?" asked Theotokis.

"A contest of speed, strength and brains," said Old Evripidis.

"Hmm, interesting. And what's the... how do you say it... the prize?"

"Do you see that tree with the bananas?"

"Yes."

"Ah," said Theotokis. "Makes sense. Makes sense."

Old Evripidis had found them a very, very difficult course. It was enormous!

"The race starts here," he said. "But no cheating! You must never touch the ground. You go from tree to tree, any tree you like, and whoever reaches the finish first wins."

"Three, two, one... GO!"

Rillas swung from one tree to the next, and King Kong was right beside him. Rillas went faster. King Kong went faster too. This way, that way! They were both incredibly fast. Whatever one did, the other did too.

Then, CRACK! A branch snapped and they both dropped! But they were very experienced gorillas. They grabbed the branches below just in time and never touched the ground.

They knew exactly where to go. At the very end there was just one metre between them... and they crossed the finish at exactly the same moment.

"It's a tie!" said Old Evripidis.

Nobody in the jungle had ever seen anything like it.

"We're both very good," they said.

"So what's next?" said Rillas. "Hurry up, I'm hungry."

"Hungry?" said King Kong. "You're going to lose!"

"We'll see about that! I'm going to flatten you!"

"Now, the test of strength," said Old Evripidis. "We have some very big animals here. You must lift a zebra, a giraffe, a rhino, a hippo and an elephant. One at a time! Whoever lifts all five is the winner."

First, the zebras. "One, two, three!" They both lifted their zebras at exactly the same time.

Next, the giraffes. "Three, two, one!" Up went both giraffes, together.

Then the rhinos. "One, two, three!" The rhinos were much heavier, and they struggled... but they both did it.

Oh my goodness, now it was the hippos' turn. "This is getting hard," thought Rillas. "Uh-oh," said King Kong, "we're in trouble."

"Three, two, one!" Wobbling and puffing, they both lifted their hippos.

"Oh my!" said Old Evripidis. "The final round! Whoever lifts the elephant is the winner!"

"Three, two, one!" Rillas went first. "Mmmmmmm..." The elephant didn't budge!

"Oh no, we're going to lose!"

King Kong tried. "Mmmmmmm..." The elephant didn't budge!

They tried once, twice, three times... but neither of them could lift the elephant.

"Ding ding ding! It's a tie!"

Only the brain test was left.

"Oh my goodness, oh my goodness!"

"I will ask each of you one question," said Old Evripidis. "Both questions are just as hard. Whoever gets the right answer wins."

"Rillas, you're first. Here is your question: John's dad has three children. One is called July, the other is called June, and the third is called...?"

"Easy! August!" said Rillas.

"Oh no!" cried Theotokis. "He lost! He lost! He got it wrong!"

"The right answer is John! It's John's dad, remember?"

Then Old Evripidis turned to King Kong.

"So, Mister King Kong. You are driving a bus. Ten passengers get on. Five get off. Three more get on. Then fifteen get on."

Poor King Kong counted and counted on his fingers...

"Now tell me: how old is the bus driver?"

King Kong's brain short-circuited. "Ummm... fifty?"

"No, no! The driver is as old as you are, because YOU are the driver!"

"I'm sorry, boys, you have both lost! So now you have two choices. Either you share the bananas... or we have one last contest."

Rillas thought. King Kong thought. They glared at each other.

"I'm not giving up," thought Rillas. "I'd rather stay hungry. But if I win, I get ALL the bananas."

"I'm not giving up either," thought King Kong. "I want ALL the bananas... even though right now I could have half."

They thought and thought. But they were both too proud to give in.

"ONE MORE CONTEST!" they shouted together.

"Ah," sighed Old Evripidis. "Ah, you never learn, you never learn... Very well. Your final contest will be... a contest of silence!"

"Wow! How does that work?"

"You sit here," said Old Evripidis, "and whoever speaks first... loses."

"Alright, alright."

The contest began, and night fell. The two gorillas sat there in silence, and everyone else went home to sleep. But they knew that whoever spoke would lose, and whoever fell asleep would lose too. So they stayed awake the whole night long.

When the sun came up, they looked up at the tree... and the bunch of bananas was GONE!

"WHAT HAPPENED?!" they both shouted at the same time.

And so, they both lost.

Then Old Evripidis arrived, carrying the bunch of bananas.

"Because you didn't cooperate," he said, "when you could each have had half the bananas, now you will each get just one banana. And we will share the rest with everyone."

Rillas and King Kong were very embarrassed, and they agreed. Instead of fifty bananas each, because they had been so greedy, they got just one banana each.

Theotokis and Aristotelis liked that very much, because Old Evripidis shared the bananas with everyone. And Rillas and King Kong finally learned their lesson: it is better to share than to be selfish.

And they lived happily ever after, and we lived even better!

The End.
